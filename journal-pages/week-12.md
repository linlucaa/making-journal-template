# Week 12: 

## Project Statement: Passive Digital Behaviour and Loss of Focus

This project is an interactive data-driven visualisation that translates personal phone-use habits into a visual representation of attention, distraction, and behavioural instability. Rather than displaying screen-time statistics through graphs or dashboards, the work visualises how different patterns of digital behaviour can influence an individual's ability to maintain focus.

Users enter information about their daily phone use across categories such as Study/Work, Planning and Organisation, Content Consumption, Gaming, and Control Over Attention. These inputs act as the project's data source and directly influence the behaviour of a dynamic particle system. Purposeful activities contribute to structured and stable visual behaviour, while passive activities introduce movement, instability, and gradual collapse. Through interaction, users can temporarily restore order, reflecting the ongoing cycle of focus, distraction, and recovery that characterises everyday digital life.

The project responds to a future in which digital technologies increasingly shape how attention is distributed and managed. While many existing tools measure behaviour through numerical metrics such as screen time, productivity scores, or usage statistics, these systems often fail to communicate the emotional and behavioural consequences of digital consumption. This visualisation critiques that reduction of human behaviour into data points by translating personal habits into a more experiential and affective form.

Rather than encouraging productivity optimisation or providing behavioural advice, the work invites reflection. By witnessing the visual effects of their own habits, users are encouraged to consider how passive digital consumption may influence their concentration, self-control, and sense of agency. The project aims to make the often invisible relationship between digital behaviour and attention more tangible, transforming personal data into an experience that can be observed, felt, and questioned.
