# Week 8 — Critique, Reflection, and Direction Refinement

## Introduction

This week focused on using critique, reflection, and continued experimentation to refine the direction of my Data-Driven Visualisation project.

## Feedback and Discussion

One of the strongest pieces of feedback was that the instability system communicated emotional tension more effectively than literal data representation.

Several people commented that:
- the movement behaviour felt increasingly anxious as instability increased,
- the collapse effect visually communicated loss of control,
- and the interaction became more engaging when users could temporarily restore order.

However, feedback also identified several areas needing refinement.
These included:
some visual behaviour becoming too chaotic at higher values,
the hierarchy between purposeful and passive systems becoming unclear,
and the interaction controls needing stronger explanation.

## Reflection on Critique

The critique process helped shift my thinking away from creating a visually complex prototype and toward building a clearer behavioural experience. One major realisation was that simplification often creates stronger communication.
Earlier versions relied heavily on: large numbers of particles, intense movement, and visual complexity.

However, feedback consistently suggested that the most effective moments occurred when:
movement became more controlled, hierarchy became easier to read, and interaction directly reinforced the project concept.

This made me reconsider how much information the visualisation actually needed in order to communicate distraction and behavioural instability effectively.

The critique also reinforced that the project should prioritise emotional readability, interaction clarity and conceptual coherence


## Direction Refinement

Following critique and testing, I spent time reflecting on how effectively the prototype communicated its intended ideas.

One recurring question was whether the project communicated distraction clearly enough through behaviour alone. While the instability and collapse systems were functioning technically, I wanted to ensure that users could understand the relationship between passive digital behaviour and loss of focus without needing extensive explanation.

This led me to think more critically about pacing, interaction, and behavioural transitions. Rather than continually adding new visual effects, I began focusing on how existing systems could be simplified and refined.

In particular, I became increasingly interested in the relationship between collapse and recovery. Earlier versions focused primarily on increasing instability and loss of control. However, through testing and reflection, I realised that the project became more engaging when users could temporarily restore focus before distraction gradually returned.
This shift encouraged me to think about attention as a cycle rather than a one-directional process. Instead of moving directly from order to collapse, the visualisation began exploring moments of focus, distraction, recovery, and relapse.

## Independent Study — Project Development

Following critique and reflection, I continued refining the prototype with a stronger focus on clarity and intentionality.

The main developments included separating instability systems more clearly, improving slider behaviour, refining movement smoothing, and introducing stronger interaction feedback.

I also improved visual hierarchy, spacing, colour balance, and behavioural relationships between systems.

One important technical improvement was separating passive intensity, movement instability, and behavioural spread into independent systems rather than allowing them to overlap too heavily.
This created clearer interaction behaviour and improved the readability of the visualisation.

## Technical Reflection

This week helped me better understand how interaction systems communicate meaning through behaviour rather than static visuals.

I learned that movement, pacing, instability and responsiveness can communicate emotional states more effectively than simply adding more visual detail.

I also realised that controlling complexity is just as important as generating complexity.
Many earlier experiments became visually noisy because multiple systems were competing simultaneously. Separating systems more clearly produced stronger communication and made the interaction easier to understand.



## Reflective Summary

The most significant outcome of Week 8 was the shift toward intentional refinement rather than continuous expansion.

Critique and testing revealed that the strongest aspects of the project were emotional movement behaviour, instability systems, recovery interactions and the contrast between purposeful and passive activity.

The weakest aspects were excessive visual noise, inconsistent hierarchy and overlapping behaviours.

As a result, my next development phase will focus on clearer behavioural systems, stronger interaction design, simplified visual communication and more intentional pacing.

The project is gradually shifting from a general visualisation about screen behaviour into a more focused interactive experience exploring distraction, behavioural instability, and temporary recovery.
