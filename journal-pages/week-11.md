## 2. Three Key Project Moments

### Moving Beyond Screen-Time Statistics

One of the most important developments in the project was realising that simply displaying screen-time data was not enough to communicate the effects of digital behaviour. Early ideas focused on visualising usage patterns and statistics, but over time I became more interested in representing the experience of distraction itself. This shifted the project from a traditional data visualisation towards a more experiential and behavioural approach.

### Adding Focus Recovery

The introduction of the focus recovery interaction significantly improved the project concept. Initially, the system only moved in one direction, with purposeful behaviour gradually collapsing under passive digital activity. By allowing users to temporarily restore focus through interaction, the visualisation began reflecting more realistic patterns of attention, distraction, and recovery. This created a more dynamic and relatable experience.

### Replacing Abstract Sliders with Personal Phone-Use Data

The most significant change occurred when I replaced the original abstract controls with categories based on personal phone-use behaviour. Instead of adjusting generic values such as instability or collapse, users could input information relating to study, planning, content consumption, gaming, and attention control. This transformed the visualisation into a more personal and meaningful experience by directly connecting user behaviour to the generated visual outcome.

## 3. Final Artefact Development

This week focused on finalising the visualisation and preparing it for submission and public display. Rather than introducing new systems, I concentrated on refining the strongest aspects of the project and improving the overall user experience.

One of the most significant changes was replacing the original abstract controls with categories based on phone-use behaviour. Earlier versions relied on sliders controlling concepts such as instability and collapse, but these variables felt disconnected from everyday experience. The final version instead uses categories such as Study/Work, Planning/Organisation, Content Consumption, Gaming, and Control Over Attention, creating a clearer relationship between user behaviour and visual output.

I also redesigned the interface to improve readability and presentation. A cleaner card-based layout was introduced, along with improved spacing and visual hierarchy. This helped separate the data input area from the visualisation itself and made the system easier to understand at a glance.

Additional refinements included introducing a state badge to communicate the current attention state, adding a pressure bar to visualise distraction levels, improving particle scaling to create smoother transitions between states, and refining the overall movement behaviour of the system.

The mouse recovery interaction was retained from earlier iterations because it remained one of the strongest aspects of the project. This interaction communicates that attention is not permanently lost and can be temporarily restored through intentional action.

These changes helped transform the prototype into a more coherent, readable, and meaningful final artefact while strengthening the relationship between personal behaviour and visual representation.

## 4. Project Statement Development

This week I refined the final project statement to ensure it clearly communicated the purpose, data source, critical perspective, and intended impact of the visualisation.

One area of focus was improving the explanation of what the project actually is. Earlier drafts described the visualisation primarily in technical terms, whereas later versions focused more clearly on the experience being created for users. The statement now describes the project as an interactive data-driven visualisation that translates phone-use behaviour into a visual representation of attention, distraction, and behavioural instability.

I also clarified the role of data within the project. Rather than using large datasets or conventional screen-time statistics, the visualisation uses user-entered phone-use categories including study, planning, content consumption, gaming, and attention control. This data directly influences the visual behaviour of the system.

Another important refinement was strengthening the critical perspective of the project. The statement now more clearly explains that the work critiques traditional screen-time dashboards, which often reduce behaviour to numbers and graphs. Instead, the project focuses on communicating the emotional and behavioural consequences of passive digital consumption through movement, instability, and collapse.

Finally, I refined the intended impact section to emphasise reflection. Rather than providing advice or measuring productivity, the visualisation encourages users to consider how their own digital habits may influence attention, focus, and self-control. These refinements helped create a clearer and more coherent project statement that better represents the goals of the final artefact.

## 5. Showcase Planning

Preparation for the showcase focused on ensuring the project could be presented clearly and effectively within a public setting.

The final setup includes:

1. a laptop displaying the p5.js visualisation in fullscreen,
2. a charger and power access,
3. a printed or digitally displayed project statement,
4. supporting screenshots and GIFs within the journal,
5. and a designated display location within the showcase layout.

Because the project relies on audience interaction, it was important that users could easily understand how to engage with the visualisation. The final interface was therefore simplified and organised to improve accessibility and readability during public display.

Week 11 focused on finalising the artefact, project statement, and journal. The project now feels more coherent because the visualisation directly responds to user behaviour rather than abstract variables.

Looking back across the development process, the most significant improvements came from simplifying the interaction and strengthening the relationship between personal behaviour and visual outcomes. The project gradually evolved from a generic particle simulation into a personalised data-driven visualisation that encourages reflection on digital habits and attention.

My final priority is preparing the work for public display and ensuring that the interaction remains clear, accessible, and engaging for viewers during the Week 12 showcase.
