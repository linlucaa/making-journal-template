# Week 10 — Critique, Evaluation, and Project Planning

## Introduction

This week focused on reviewing the current state of my Data-Driven Visualisation project and identifying the most important areas for improvement before final submission. Through critique, reflection, and project evaluation, I began assessing how effectively the prototype communicated its core ideas about passive digital behaviour and attentional instability.

At this stage, the project consisted of an interactive p5.js visualisation where passive digital behaviour gradually destabilised an otherwise structured system through movement, collapse, and visual instability. While the interaction was functioning successfully, I wanted to determine whether the visualisation was communicating meaningful ideas or simply producing interesting visual effects.

## Feedback and Reflection

One of the most useful outcomes of this week was reflecting on how people interpreted the visualisation.

The strongest aspects identified were:

1. the contrast between purposeful and passive behaviour,
2. the collapse of structure over time,
3. and the temporary focus recovery interaction.

Several people commented that the prototype successfully communicated feelings of distraction, instability, and loss of control without requiring detailed explanation.

However, feedback also highlighted several areas requiring further development.

These included:

1. improving visual clarity,
2. simplifying interface elements,
3. strengthening the relationship between interaction and meaning,
4. and reducing unnecessary complexity.

Through these discussions, I began questioning whether all aspects of the visualisation were contributing equally to the overall concept.

## Action Plan

The most useful feedback I received was that the project became much stronger when it focused on communicating the experience of distraction rather than simply displaying screen-time information. Users responded positively to the instability, collapse, and recovery mechanics because they could immediately understand the relationship between passive behaviour and loss of focus.

Before Week 12, I need to continue refining the visual communication of the project. My main priorities are improving the clarity of the interface, strengthening the relationship between user interaction and system behaviour, and ensuring that the progression from focus to distraction is easy to understand. I also need to improve the overall presentation quality by replacing placeholder images, documenting the final prototype more thoroughly, and preparing visual material for the showcase.

I will not spend time introducing entirely new features or rebuilding the project from scratch. User feedback consistently showed that the core concept is already working, so adding unnecessary complexity could weaken the communication of the idea. Instead, I will focus on refinement, usability, and presentation.

By Week 12, I aim to have a fully polished prototype, complete documentation of the development process, a final project statement, high-quality screenshots and GIFs demonstrating the visualisation, and a presentation-ready version suitable for public display at the showcase.

## Prototype Refinement

Following critique feedback, I refined the prototype to improve clarity, readability, and communication.

Changes included:

1. increasing contrast between purposeful and passive behaviours,
2. improving movement smoothing,
3. reducing visual clutter,
4. strengthening behavioural transitions,
5. refining interface labels,
6. and improving the focus recovery interaction.

These changes focused on improving communication rather than adding entirely new functionality.

## Reflection

This week was primarily about evaluation rather than expansion. The critique process helped identify what was already working within the project and what still needed refinement before submission.

One of the most important insights was that strong communication comes from clarity rather than complexity. The feedback reinforced that the project should prioritise behavioural meaning, interaction quality, and visual readability rather than introducing additional systems.

Moving forward, I will focus on refining the strongest aspects of the project and preparing the visualisation for final presentation and public display.

## Independent Study — Project Development

Outside of class, I continued reviewing feedback, refining interaction systems, and documenting prototype progress through screenshots and reflection notes.

I also spent time planning how the final project would be presented at the showcase, considering what visual material, documentation, and explanations would best communicate the project to an unfamiliar audience.

A major focus moving forward will be ensuring that the final visualisation feels deliberate, understandable, and emotionally engaging while maintaining a strong connection to the project’s central critique of passive digital behaviour.
