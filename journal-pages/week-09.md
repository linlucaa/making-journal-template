# Week 9 — Project Statement, Focused Development, and Direction Commitment

## Introduction

This week focused on committing to a clearer final direction for my Data-Driven Visualisation project through project statement drafting and focused prototype development.

The project continues exploring the relationship between intentional digital behaviour, passive consumption, behavioural instability, and loss of control through interactive systems.

Using p5.js, I have been developing an interactive visualisation where purposeful structures gradually destabilise as passive digital activity increases. Rather than representing screen data literally, the project focuses on communicating behavioural and emotional experiences through movement, interaction, and system collapse.

The main goal this week was to define the project concept more clearly, strengthen the critical framing, and commit to a more refined interaction direction before the final stages of development.

## 1. Project Statement — First Draft

The first activity focused on drafting an early version of the project statement. Before writing my own statement, I analysed the case study *Xeno Computer 0.1: Labor* by Tega Brain and Sam Lavigne, which explored ideas surrounding data, labour, automation, and power.

This helped me think more critically about:

- how data visualisations communicate meaning,
- how interaction can provoke emotional reactions,
- and how projects can critique systems rather than simply display information.

I then used previous journal entries, reflective proposal work, visual references, and project documentation to generate and refine a first draft project statement.

The drafting process helped clarify:

- what the visualisation actually represents,
- what behavioural ideas it critiques,
- and what emotional response I want viewers to experience.

## Evaluating the Draft

After generating the statement draft, I critically evaluated which parts felt:

- clear,
- underdeveloped,
- overly general,
- or too AI-generated.

One issue I identified was that some sections described the project too technically instead of explaining the emotional and behavioural experience of the interaction.

Another issue was that earlier wording focused too heavily on screen time as data rather than the broader psychological effects of passive digital behaviour.

The strongest parts of the draft were the sections discussing:

- instability,
- behavioural collapse,
- distraction cycles,
- and the contrast between intentional and passive interaction.

This evaluation process helped me commit more strongly to the idea that the project is not just visualising data, but critically exploring how digital systems shape attention and behaviour.

## Direction Commitment

One important outcome from this week was writing a sentence that clearly committed to the project direction.

My committed direction became:

> "This project uses interactive behavioural systems to visualise how passive digital consumption gradually destabilises intentional focus and emotional control."

Writing this sentence helped simplify the project concept and made future development decisions easier.

- what needed the most development,

- and what behaviours I wanted to test.

The sprint focused primarily on:

- improving movement smoothing,

Rather than adding completely new systems, this sprint focused on refinement and control.

## Technical Development

One of the biggest technical improvements this week was separating the relationship between:

- passive intensity,
- instability,
- and spread behaviour.

Earlier versions caused these systems to overlap too heavily, which reduced readability and made the visualisation feel random rather than intentional.

The updated system now allows:

- passive intensity to control quantity,
- instability to control movement behaviour,
- and spread to control visual contamination between systems.

This created a much clearer behavioural hierarchy and made interaction feel more understandable.

I also added:

- smoother movement transitions,
- cleaner spacing,
- and temporary recovery interactions using mouse input.

These changes made the visualisation feel more dynamic and emotionally responsive.

## Reflection and Testing

Throughout development, I continued sharing the prototype with classmates and other users to gather reactions and identify areas for improvement.

Many people interpreted the purposeful system as representing focus, routine, and intentional behaviour, while the passive system was associated with distraction, overload, and loss of control.

One useful observation was that the interaction successfully communicated stress and behavioural tension even without extensive explanation. However, feedback also suggested that the visual hierarchy could be clearer and that recovery interactions could be strengthened to make the behavioural cycle easier to understand.

Several people also noted that simpler interactions were generally more effective than highly complex visual behaviour. This reinforced my growing belief that the project would benefit more from refinement and simplification than from adding additional systems.

These reactions helped guide future development and provided useful insight into how viewers interpreted the visualisation without needing detailed instructions.

## Reflection

This week helped transform the project from a collection of experiments into a more focused critical visualisation.

The project statement process forced me to think more carefully about:

- what the project is actually arguing,
- what emotional impact it creates,
- and what ideas about digital behaviour it critiques.

The making sprint also reinforced that refinement is currently more valuable than expansion.

Instead of continuously adding systems, the project improves most when:

- movement becomes more intentional,
- hierarchy becomes clearer,
- and interaction directly supports the behavioural concept.

The feedback and testing process was especially useful because it revealed how viewers emotionally interpreted the interaction without needing detailed explanation.

This showed that the visual behaviour itself is beginning to successfully communicate:

- instability,
- distraction,
- pressure,
- and temporary recovery.
